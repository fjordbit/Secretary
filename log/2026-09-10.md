**Subject:** getting operational data off the shiplift PLCs and into a

Full structured notes, with the interpretation marked:

### What was described

- Today: TIA Portal produces running hours, collected into a local SQL database.
  Everything stays on site. Some customers permit data leaving; some do not.
- Target: **EG Maintenance**, a cloud, object-based, packaged maintenance
  system. Packaged means the integration bends to it.
- Pipeline: TIA → local PC → SQL → **operator confirms "docking complete"** →
  send → EG Maintenance → report to the customer.
- On-change logging only, deadband per signal. 8 bytes analogue, 10 bytes per
  hoist digital, 4 to 50 hoists per installation.
- Address mapping between PLC IO and maintenance objects is workable because
  Syncrolift's POS numbering and hoist addressing are standardised. Load cell
  types are not, and must be populated per installation.
- Wanted beyond running hours: **overload data**; **wire rope and sheave load**
  with fleet angle and elapsed days. Ramin has calculations.

### What I flagged back

- **The bandwidth argument does not hold at these volumes.** A kilobyte per
  docking is nothing. If the constraint is real it is *connectivity* — no link,
  satellite, or a customer firewall — not volume. Worth separating, because the
  two lead to different designs. On-change logging stays right regardless, but
  for storage and signal quality.
- **"Usage-based" is the wrong frame on its own.** Stian's own note contains the
  counter-example: saltwater in the sheaves. Idle equipment in seawater
  corrodes; a sheave that does not turn does not shed water or move grease. The
  interval needs running hours *and* calendar time *and* environment. This is
  the point most likely to be challenged in front of management.
