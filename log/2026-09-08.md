## What this changes in the strategy paper

Three places, in order of how much they matter:

1. **Where the value actually sits (sections 8–9).** The gain model is built on
   project 30197 — a *won, delivered* project. But if thousands of tenders go
   out and 30+ ever became shiplifts, the overwhelming majority of offer-stage
   work is spent on tenders that never convert. The workflow being built is
   enquiry-to-offer. It therefore automates precisely the part of the business
   that is mostly unrecovered cost. That is a **stronger** argument than the one
   the paper currently makes, and the paper does not make it at all.

2. **The addressable-hours base (section 9).** 17 staff × 1 600 h × 60 % =
   ≈ 16 300 addressable hours is described as *project* hours. Open question:
   does that pool include tender and bid work, or only delivered projects? If
   tender work sits outside it, the paper is understating the gain and putting
   it in the wrong place. This compounds assumptions-review item 4, which
   already flags 17 and 60 % as unverified.

3. **The evaluation set and the gate (sections 7 and 12).** The paper says
   "past enquiries with known good outcomes". With thousands of tenders and 30+
   wins, "good outcome" is ambiguous — offers that were *sent* (thousands; ample
   data) or offers that *won* (30+; thin). The gate criterion "engineering
   accepts the output without rework on at least X % of the evaluation set"
   only works against the first. Worth stating explicitly so nobody reads the
   gate as a win-rate test.

Also: the example workflow in section 3 lists "Bill of material" and "Cost
calculation" as separate outputs. Given the BOM gap above, the generated BOM
should carry a price column.

## What CATIA / 3DEXPERIENCE changes

4. **Step 4 of the example workflow is the least-specified step in the paper.**
   Section 3 marks "Drawings and documents — generates the arrangement and
   interface drawings from the configuration" as *Automated*. On 3DEXPERIENCE
   that is not a model-writes-a-drawing task; it is knowledge-based engineering
   against CATIA templates and the platform's APIs. It is likely the largest
   single piece of build work in project 02, and the paper gives it one line.
   Work package 4 (weeks 12–28) should say so.

5. **Possible tension with the IP argument (section 5).** The architecture rests
   on tier-2 engineering content and tier-3 methods never leaving Syncrolift.
   3DEXPERIENCE can be deployed on Dassault's cloud. If Syncrolift's PLM data
   already sits there, "never leaves the building" needs rewording — the honest
   version is that it never goes to a *model vendor*, and that Dassault is an
   existing, contracted data processor. Better to say this before someone in
   IT or QHSE says it. **To check:** is their 3DEXPERIENCE on-premises or cloud?

## What the existing VB layer changes

6. **Assumptions-review item 1 is answered, and it was the highest-risk one.**
   The paper assumed bilge-arm sizing and design rules *can be* encoded as
   deterministic on-premises tools, at medium confidence, with "ask the P-2003
   owner what the calculation actually is" as the verification step. They are
   already encoded — parameterised in CATIA and driven from outside by Ramin's
   VB app. Confidence goes to high, and the paper should say so plainly.

7. **Work package 3 shrinks (weeks 6–20, "encode bilge-arm sizing and design
   rules as on-premises tools").** Much of that is wrapping and validating an
   existing system, not building one. That is the single biggest de-risking fact
   available for the meeting, and it strengthens the schedule argument.

8. **It also names the real dependency.** The workflow's leverage on step 2 and
   step 4 runs through the VB layer. Its maintainability — held largely in the
   code and in one person — becomes a programme risk, not just a tidiness
   concern. This is the honest case for the modern-framework rewrite, and it
   belongs in section 11 alongside "single point of failure in the architect
   role", which is the same risk pointed at a different person.

## Scope watch

9. **The systems mess is integration work, not AI work.** Two after-sales
    systems that don't talk, and a spare-parts API between two more, are plain
    integration. The risk is that they get attached to Stian because his is the
    nearest thing to a digital initiative, and project 02 slips to pay for it.
    This is deck defect #4 — one role, two jobs — coming back in a new form,
    and the portfolio split in section 6 exists precisely to stop it.
    It is still worth knowing about: section 5 promises connectors to business
    systems, and a spare-parts data mess is a data-quality risk for the vessel
    database (01) and archive mining (04). Know it; don't own it.

10. **TIA Portal Openness with agents is the same pattern as CATIA, not a twin
    problem.** Openness is an external API driving an engineering tool
    programmatically — architecturally identical to the parameterised CATIA
    models driven by Ramin's VB layer: the agent orchestrates, a deterministic
    tool does the work. Filing it under project 05 (controls, monitoring,
    virtual twin) is defensible for the *twin*, but the agent-testing part is
    platform work, closer to 02/03. Worth deciding deliberately rather than
    letting it merge, because the paper's whole portfolio argument is that these
    lanes get resourced and reported separately.

## Price prediction thinking

Stian dumped a substantial design for part-level price prediction with
uncertainty. Written up separately as
`Prosjekter/agentic/04_Price_Prediction_Concept.md`.

Short version: the idea is sound and the per-part framing dissolves the
"30 projects is not enough data" objection, because the unit of observation is
the part, not the shiplift. It is not an LLM problem at all — it is classical
regression, which means it slots into the paper as a tier-3 on-premises tool.
Five corrections noted in the concept note, the important ones being: hold out
whole projects rather than random parts, and do not aggregate part
probabilities as if they were independent.

cannot be settled before someone looks at the data — he said as much himself.
