# The 40 mm head on rope of 38 to 40 mm

Calculated, not measured. 2D axisymmetric model, homogenised rope, placeholder yoke steel, large model box
(r 2400 × z ±2400 mm). The head is the model's H40 head; it is not the prototype head on the rig (see §5). The head
and its elements stay where they are; only the rope changes. Every lift-off is measured from the surface of a
40 mm rope. Both magnet grades, N52 and N48SH, are in every table and figure. Nothing here is selected.

**In plain words.**
1. A 38 mm rope in the 40 mm head carries slightly more flux per square millimetre of metal than a 40 mm rope,
   about 0.014 T more in the middle of the head, because the same magnets drive the flux through less metal.
2. In the middle of the head the rope stays between 1.86 and 2.07 T in every case run (38 to 41.6 mm, both
   grades, three rope curves, −40 to +45 °C), so it never comes near 2.3 T there.
3. Next to the magnets, 45 mm from the middle, the rope runs harder, 2.16 to 2.36 T: it passes 2.3 T with N52 on
   the high rope curve at every temperature, and at −40 °C with N52 on the middle curve and N48SH on the high
   curve; the rope size hardly changes that.
4. On the low rope curve the middle of the head is at or just under 1.9 T at 25 and 45 °C (1.865 to 1.905 T);
   the middle curve sits inside 1.9 to 2.0 T, the high curve above it.
5. A groove cut so that the axial element at 16 mm sits on the zero with a 40 mm rope leaves about −6 mT of
   standing field at that element with a 38 mm rope, and the zero moves out to about 18 mm.
6. A groove about 0.4 mm deeper centres the zero for 38 mm instead; a groove for 39 mm leaves −3 to −4 mT at 38 mm
   and +2 to +4 mT at 40 mm, inside the element's range of about ±20 mT on its own; with a passing loss, a rope
   resting on one side and build tolerances added, the margin is not shown here (§3).
7. With a 38 mm rope the signals are smaller: about a quarter less for a long loss of metal at the axial element,
   and for a short fault at the radial element 3 mm out, because the rope sits 1 mm further away and has less metal.
8. If the 38 mm rope rests on one side, the element on that side reads about as much as on a 40 mm rope and the
   element opposite a third to over 40 % less; this last point is an approximation, not a 3D run.

## Dense part

### 1. Model and what was solved

Solver, mesh rules (refine 0.25, pole lines), Newton tolerance (1e-11) and read-outs as `../falsify/g_lib.py`
(imported unchanged); box r 2400 × z ±2400 mm with A = 0 on it, the converged box of `../falsify/g_large_box.md`
§1. 2D axisymmetric: the rope is centred in every solve.

- **Head.** H40 of `../../params.yaml`: bore radius 24 mm, guard 2 mm, magnet rings r 26–43.2 mm, 40 mm long at
  z ±50 … ±90 mm, radially magnetised and opposed, recoil permeability 1.05; yoke tube r 43.2–51.71 mm (8.511 mm)
  over |z| ≤ 90 mm; outer groove 20 mm wide at the mid-plane. Yoke steel: the placeholder table of `params.yaml`.
- **Grades.** N52 1.42 T, N48SH 1.36 T: register minimum remanence at 25 °C, the values of the trade-off run
  (branch u107-tradeoff-map, `tradeoff/t_net.py`) and the groove figures (branch u106-groove-plots,
  `groove_plots/run_missing.py`). Applied as in `groove_plots/run_missing.py`: the remanence of the modelled
  magnet replaced, its size kept (17.2 mm). The trade-off run made a stronger magnet thicker instead; here the
  size is the H40 size, so that the 40 mm head is the 40 mm head.
- **Temperature.** The trade-off run was at 25 °C only, so its handling is the tool's own rule
  (`params.yaml` magnet.alpha_br, the same as `../demo/groove_physics.py` part sens and `../falsify/f_magnet_mech.py`):
  Br(T) = Br25 (1 − 0.0012 (T − 25)), the same coefficient for both grades. −40 °C: Br × 1.078; +45 °C: × 0.976.
  The rope and steel curves do not change with temperature in this tool (`params.yaml` rope.temperature_dependence).
  The +45 °C end is the highest service temperature in the project's record.
- **Rope curves.** Low / middle / high of `../demo/h52_groove.py` BRACKET (Bs 1.90 / 1.97 / 2.03 T, Ha 2500 / 1700 /
  1300 A/m; the wire law B = Bs (2/π) atan(H/Ha) + μ0H), as `../falsify/g_large_box.py` part wp.
- **Rope size and fill.** d = 38, 39, 40 mm and 41.6 mm (40 mm + 4 %). Fill 0.7270 at every d: `params.yaml`
  rope.fill = metallic area factor C 0.571 (rope maker's catalogue value in the register, [R9]) ÷ π/4. The
  metallic area C·d² is therefore 824.5 / 868.5 / 913.6 / 988.2 mm²: it scales with d². Rope induction = flux
  through the rope disc ÷ that area.
- **Read-outs.** Standing B_z on the mid-plane every 0.1 mm of radius from 0.5 mm off the rope to 0.1 mm short of
  the magnet face; the zero = first sign change; read at fixed radii (r = 20 mm + lift-off). Rope induction at the
  mid-plane and the largest along |z| ≤ 90 mm (`gp.circuit`). Long loss: 300 mm of rope with 10 % of its own
  metal lost, centred on the head (z_d 0 only, as the trade-off run; the mean over z_d ±30 mm of the groove notes is
  0.7 % smaller at the H52 reference point), (damaged − intact) ÷ 10 on one mesh. Short fault: ring 4 mm long, 30 %
  of the metal lost in the outer 2 mm of the rope, at z_d 0; |ΔB_r| read at heights 0–14 mm along the rope at each
  radius and the largest kept (as the trade-off run, which checked this against moving the fault: within 2.5 %).
- **Read-out scatter.** B_z is read with the 1 mm difference stencil of all the groove notes. The falsifier read
  the same solves with a 0.05 mm stencil: the standing B_z at 36 mm moved by up to about 1.8 mT and the zero by up
  to 0.3 mm (the profile is noisy at element scale). Take the standing B_z as ±1–2 mT and the zero as ±0.3 mm in
  absolute terms; the two-decimal values are the 1 mm read-out, not that precise. Differences between ropes on the
  same read-out (about 2.9 mT and 1 mm of zero per mm of diameter) are larger than that scatter.
- **Solves.** Depth search 58 solves (secant on depth, |zero − 16| < 0.02 mm, all 18 met within 2–4 rounds);
  standing 240 (three depths × four diameters × three temperatures, and no groove at 25 °C, per grade and curve);
  signals 80. All 378 converged (11–14 Newton steps, about 160–218 k nodes). Logs with local paths are not kept.

Files: `h40_run.py` (parts depth, stand, signals), `h40_summary.py` (read-outs, prints the tables),
`h40_figures.py`; `results/depth.json`, `stand.json`, `signals.json`, `summary.json`. `plotstandard.py` and
`figkit.py` are copies, unchanged, of `max/sws/3d/groove_plots/` on branch u106-groove-plots (commit d112319),
which is not on main.

### 2. Rope induction (item 1)

The groove is at the 40 mm depth of each grade and curve (2.29–2.81 mm). Going from 40 to 38 mm raises the
mid-plane induction by +0.014 T (all grades, curves, temperatures: +0.013 … +0.015 T), 39 mm by +0.007 T; 41.6 mm
lowers it by −0.012 … −0.014 T. The flux through the rope falls with the smaller rope (about −9.1 % at 38 mm),
a little less than its metallic area (−9.75 %). −40 °C against +25 °C: +0.011 … +0.013 T; +45 °C: −0.004 T. N52 against N48SH: +0.006 T.
The rope curve moves it most: low / high against middle −0.085 / +0.067 T.

Against 1.9–2.0 T: the middle curve is inside at all four diameters and temperatures, except N52 at 38 mm and
−40 °C (2.000 T, on the edge). The low curve is below 1.9 T at 40 mm at every temperature for N48SH and at 25 / 45 °C
for N52; at 38 mm it is below only for N48SH at 25 / 45 °C (1.898 / 1.894 T) — so a 38 mm rope does not push it
under 1.9 T; it lifts it slightly. The high curve is above 2.0 T everywhere (2.019–2.067 T). Without a groove
(25 °C) the mid-plane is 0.009–0.025 T higher (`no_groove_25C`).

Against 2.3 T: the mid-plane never. The peak along the rope sits at z ±45 mm, 5 mm inside the inner edge of each
magnet ring (checked on N52, middle curve, −40 °C, 38 and 40 mm: z 45.0 mm both); it is 0.23–0.36 T above the
mid-plane and changes by at most 0.004 T between 38 and 41.6 mm. Past 2.3 T: N52 high curve at all three
temperatures (2.302–2.362 T), N52 middle at −40 °C (2.309–2.311 T), N48SH high at −40 °C (2.335–2.336 T); just
under: N48SH high at 25 °C (2.290–2.294 T). So whether the rope passes 2.3 T is set by the rope curve and the
temperature, not by 38 against 40 mm. Only the mid-plane is the working point of the loss-of-metal reading; what a
peak past 2.3 T near the magnets does to the reading was not looked at here.

### 3. Zero and standing field at the axial element (item 2)

Element fixed at 16 mm from the 40 mm surface (r 36 mm), as the zero position used on the H52 head; the H40 groove
note also ran 10 and 13 mm, not repeated here.

- Depth for the zero at 16 mm with 40 mm rope: N52 2.81 / 2.54 / 2.34 mm, N48SH 2.79 / 2.50 / 2.29 mm (low / middle
  / high curve; the large-box H40 note value for the modelled N40SH magnet: 2.64 mm). For 38 mm: +0.41 … +0.45 mm
  deeper (N52 3.22 / 2.96 / 2.77, N48SH 3.20 / 2.93 / 2.74 mm). For 39 mm: N52 3.01 / 2.75 / 2.55, N48SH 2.99 / 2.72
  / 2.51 mm.
- With the 40 mm groove the zero lands at 17.8–18.2 mm (25 °C) for 38 mm, 16.9–17.1 mm for 39 mm and 14.2–14.5 mm for
  41.6 mm: about 1 mm of zero per mm of rope diameter, i.e. the zero stays at roughly the same distance from the
  rope surface. Standing B_z at 16 mm: −5.7 … −5.9 mT (38 mm), −2.9 … −3.0 (39), +5.0 … +5.3 (41.6) at 25 °C.
  Temperature adds −1.3 … +0.4 mT at 40 mm (−40 °C the negative side): the magnet's remanence moves the zero by
  0.3–0.6 mm over −40 … +45 °C.
- One depth for the range: with the 39 mm groove the ends read −2.8 … −4.3 mT (38 mm) and +2.1 … +3.7 mT (40 mm)
  over grades, curves and temperatures; the 4 % oversize 40 mm rope +7.6 … +9.3 mT. With the 40 mm groove the
  38 mm end is −5.5 … −7.1 mT; with the 38 mm groove the 40 mm end is +5.8 … +7.3 mT and 41.6 mm +11.6 … +13.3 mT.
  All are inside ±20 mT on their own. What else uses the same range (from earlier notes, not re-run): a 10 % long
  loss passing (10 × the level, about 4.3–6.0 mT here), the rope resting on one side (below), build tolerances
  (`../falsify/d_measurement_chain.md` §3: a worst-case sum of 12.8–21.8 mT on H52, old box). For a 38 mm rope the
  worst standing field (−7.1 mT), the rope on one side (−2.9 mT more at the nearest element) and a 10 % loss
  (−4.5 mT) add to about −14.5 mT with the 40 mm groove and about −11.7 mT with the 39 mm groove (−4.3 mT
  standing); build tolerances come on top.
- Rope on one side (38 mm, offset 1 mm): local-gap reading −8.3 … −8.8 mT at the nearest element and −2.7 … −3.1 mT
  at the farthest (centred −5.8); 39 mm, offset 0.5 mm: −4.2 … −4.4 / −1.5 … −1.8 mT. An existing 3D run on H40 with
  a 40 mm rope off centre (`../verification.md`, eccentricity, no groove, old box) moved B_z at 3 mm by 1.55 mT per
  mm of offset where the centred radial slope is about 2.0 mT per mm, so the local-gap reading may overstate the
  offset effect by about a fifth; not checked at 16 mm or with the groove.

### 4. Signals (item 3)

**4.1 Long loss at the axial element.** Level per 1 % at 16 mm, 25 °C: N52 −0.454 / −0.533 / −0.597 / −0.677 mT,
N48SH −0.432 / −0.514 / −0.586 / −0.668 mT (38 / 39 / 40 / 41.6 mm). 38 against 40 mm: −24 % (N52), −26 % (N48SH);
low and high curves: −22 % / −25 % (N52), −24 % / −27 % (N48SH). 1 % of a 38 mm rope is 9.75 % less metal than 1 %
of a 40 mm rope; the rest comes from the rope sitting further from the element. Per mm² of metal lost the 38 mm
reading is 16–18 % smaller (N52 / N48SH). The level is flat across lift-off near 16 mm (Figure 4), so the rope resting on one
side changes it by about 1 % (nearest −0.450, farthest −0.460 mT, N52). Ripple of about 0.01 mT along lift-off is
mesh scatter.

**4.2 Short fault at the radial element.** Largest |ΔB_r| (heights 0–14 mm) at 3 and 5 mm from the 40 mm surface.
Centred 38 mm: −24 % (3 mm) and −17 % (5 mm) against 40 mm, both grades; across curves −23 … −24 % at 3 mm. The
41.6 mm rope: +31 % / +17 %. The fault is the same in kind (4 mm, 30 % of the outer 2 mm) on each rope, so a
little less metal on the smaller rope.

**4.3 Rope on one side.** Not axisymmetric, so not solved. Stand-in: the centred 38 mm solution read at the
element's own gap from the rope surface — nearest element at the gap a 40 mm rope would have (offset 1 mm), the
farthest 2 mm further. Short fault, N52: nearest 11.45 mT (+7 % against 40 mm), farthest 6.13 mT (−43 %) at 3 mm;
6.13 / 3.85 mT (+7 % / −33 %) at 5 mm; N48SH the same within 1 %. This ignores how the flux redistributes round an
off-centre rope; the 3D comparison in §3 suggests it overstates the change by roughly a fifth.

**4.4 One broken outer wire.** One wire is not axisymmetric; the 3D stranded model (`../falsify/c_wire_k.py`) is
set up for H52 only and was not run. Stand-in: a ring 2 mm long (the register's break gap) whose depth is one outer
wire (2.5 mm on 52 mm rope in `../rope_construction.yaml`, scaled with d: 1.83 mm at 38, 1.92 at 40 mm) and whose loss
is one wire's area spread round that ring. Its numbers (Table 2) show how the signal moves from 40 to 38 mm: −30 %
(3 mm) and −19 % (5 mm) centred, +6 / −49 % nearest / farthest at 3 mm. One real wire's field falls off faster with
distance than a ring's: a free-space dipole at the wire centre gives (5.9 / 6.9)³ = 0.63 for 1 mm more gap at 5 mm,
i.e. about −37 % instead of the ring's −19 %, and 0.50 at 3 mm. So a 38 mm rope centred costs one wire's signal
somewhere between about 20 and 40 % at 5 mm, and the farthest element on a rope resting on one side more. For a
size: on H52 / 52 mm in 3D one outer-strand wire gave 1.17–1.36 mT at 5 mm (`../falsify/c_third_dimension.md` §2),
5.9–6.8 times the 0.20 mT the acceptance rule asks for; that H52 figure is not an H40 figure.

### 5. What the model's H40 head is and is not

It is the head of the project's parameter file and of the earlier groove notes (`../groove_physics.md`,
`../falsify/g_large_box.md` §5): magnets 17.2 × 40 mm at 50–90 mm from the mid-plane, yoke r 43.2–51.7 mm, a 20 mm
wide groove, rope homogenised, everything round. It is **not** the prototype head on the rig: that head
(`../falsify/h_prototype_head.md`) has a 105 mm yoke, r 43–51 mm with a 1 mm relief between the magnets, magnet rings 20 mm long at the yoke ends
(z 32.5–52.5 mm), a 10 mm wide, 2 mm deep groove, radial holes and end plates, and an assumed N42 magnet. The numbers
here do not carry to the prototype head as they stand; the trends (zero about 1 mm out per mm less rope diameter,
about a quarter less signal at 38 mm) are expected to be similar in kind, not checked.

### 6. Not run

3D for the off-centre rope and the single wire (stand-ins above); the 10 and 13 mm element positions; the long
loss along the rope (only centred on the head), its flatness and the summed-ring kernel; the short fault's ΔB_z;
the 39 mm groove's signals (only its standing field); demagnetisation and forces at −40 / +45 °C (the N52 knee in a
warm head is in `../falsify/f_magnet_mech.md`, H52, old box); a fill other than 0.7270 for the 38 mm rope (a rope
of another construction may have another fill); temperature dependence of the rope and steel; surroundings
(`../falsify/g_large_box.md` §6: up to several % of metal); any measurement.

### Terms

- **H40**: the model's head for 40 mm rope (§5). **N52, N48SH**: magnet grades (sintered NdFeB); register minimum
  remanence at 25 °C 1.42 / 1.36 T.
- **B_z, B_r**: the field along the rope and the field pointing away from the rope axis, in mT.
- **Axial element**: the sensor reading B_z, at 16 mm here; **radial element**: the sensor reading B_r near the rope.
- **Zero**: the lift-off where the standing B_z on the mid-plane is zero. **Standing field**: the field with an
  intact rope. **Level**: the change of B_z per 1 % of the rope's own metal lost over a long stretch.
- **Rope curve**: low / middle / high magnetisation curve of the rope wire (a bracket; the wire's real curve is not
  known). **Rope induction**: flux through the rope ÷ its metallic area.
- **Lift-off**: distance from the surface of a 40 mm rope, so the element positions do not move with the rope.
