# Release round 17 Nov: summary

**What was run.** Three language models (Grok 4.7, Fable 5.2, Sol = GPT 5.6) each designed entry, release and centring for the rope tester head (step 1). Then each cross-checked all three answers with the names removed (step 2). Step 2 had a correction: across the split the magnets **push the halves apart**. Each half is pulled onto the rope. Step 1 was written without that correction, and with a side-force figure (0.4–0.8 kN per mm off centre) that is now withdrawn.

This is the models' opinion. The mechanical designer decides.

Words: "kN" = kilonewton, about 100 kg of force. "Shear pin" = a pin made to break at a set load. "Shuttle" = a short slide that lets the whole head move a little along the rope when something drags it. "Wedge" or "cam" = a sloped track that turns that movement into a sideways push. "Gimbal" = two pivots, so the head can tilt with the rope. "Float" = a soft mount, so the head can move sideways with the rope.

## 1. In five lines

1. The models think the halves will not come off the rope by themselves. They propose a mechanism that pushes them off and latches them clear. (3 of 3)
2. They end on a captive breaking pin as the release, not a spring catch. Grok and Fable propose 10 kN, Sol 8 kN.
3. They propose that the snag's drag moves the head on its shuttle, and a wedge turns this into a push that opens the halves. Springs finish, and the halves latch and stay tied. (3 of 3) **But Fable warns: pushing the halves apart does not push both off the rope. One half can stay on. Only Fable's concept pushes the opening against something fixed.**
4. They propose a funnel at each end and no crush funnel, a head that floats and tilts with the rope, and rollers to centre it. One roller station or two is disputed.
5. First before any drawing: the force against opening gap on the 52 mm rope (Grok), the 3D magnetic model for three head sizes (Fable), a site survey of tension and rope movement (Sol).

## 2. Agreed by 3 of 3 (after the cross-check)

- **A funnel at each end, split with the halves, with no step into the bore.** A standing wire is laid down, not caught.
- **No crush funnel.** It leaves debris in the bore and leaves the head on the rope.
- **Gravity will not clear the halves.** Each half weighs about 70 N. The halves are held together with a net 550–700 N (correction).
- **The release element is a captive breaking pin.** Its load is set by its size, not by friction, and it is either whole or broken. Sol dropped its own step 1 spring catch.
- **The release load lies between 1.5 kN (one wire) and 43 kN (smallest strand catch), in both directions.** This keeps a release away from normal loads and below damaging ones.
- **The head follows the rope.** A stiff bracket fights rope tension × angle, which either trips the release by mistake or presses on the rope.
- **Rollers and funnels sit on the halves and leave with them.** Nothing is left around the rope.
- **Each half reports "closed" and "parked clear".** "Not closed" alone does not prove that a half is clear of the rope.
- **If both halves report clear: a latched alarm, the lift may finish, and the next start is blocked.**
- **Reset with the hoist stopped:** inspect that place on the rope, close with a screw tool, fit a new pin. The closing tool keeps hands out of the pinch.
- **The V-shoes (Fable's step 1) are rejected,** including by Fable. They put the rope off centre on purpose and use up the 0.5 mm budget.
- **An empty head (no rope) is not calculated and may push itself open.** Grok and Fable: the closing tool and the park latch must handle it. Sol puts no-rope cases into its test 2.

## 4. Disputed and left open

- **How fast the opening is.** Fable: nearly static, because the rope moves only 50 mm in one second at 3 m/min. Sol: the rope and bracket are springy, so a fast jolt is possible when the pin breaks. Grok: at 15 m/min, a 70 mm wedge stroke takes about 0.3 s.
- **How far clear the halves park.** Grok: at least 80 mm. Fable: more than 80 mm (rope movement plus the largest defect radius). Sol: sized from the measured movement and defect sizes.
- **Load rating of the closure and bracket.** Fable (step 1) rated bracket, shaft and wedge at 30 kN or more. Sol: that is below the 43 kN smallest strand catch, so a seized release could overload the structure first. Fable (step 2) still rates the closure at 30 kN or more until the latch dogs are withdrawn. Fable's reason for a strong closure: a swell wedged in the funnel pushes outward with about 2.5 × the axial force (at µ = 0.2, Fable's estimate), so the split must carry the 370 N push plus this "wedge burst". An ice collar at 2–3 kN would burst a lightly held head.
- **The tether's proof load.** Grok: above the pin load. Sol: it may need to approach 43 kN if a half stays caught.
- **Where the head sits.** Fable: near the upper sheave block. Grok and Sol: open.
- **Load on a strand before release.** Fable asks whether 10–15 kN on a lifted strand already counts as "further damage". Not answered.

## 5. The release, step by step (as the cross-check leaves it)

1. **What goes first.** A captive pin holds the shuttle (3 of 3). Grok and Sol: in double shear (cut in two places). Fable also has a switch at 3 mm of travel that reports "dragging" before the pin breaks.
2. **At what load (the models' estimates).**
   - Grok: 10 kN, 8–12 kN accepted. It must hold 1.5 kN and a small bunch of wires, and be gone before 43 kN. A 5 kN setting is too close to one wire plus ice. (Step 1 reasons: about 6× one wire and 4× under 43 kN; overshoot to about 15 kN still under 43 kN.)
   - Fable: about 10 kN, hold ≥6 kN, break ≤15 kN. Reason: Fable's step 1 ice estimates (2–3 kN scraping, up to 17 kN frozen on) crowd 5 kN, and nothing known needs a lower setting.
   - Sol: 8 kN, 7–9 kN. Reason: roughly equal margins to 1.5 kN and 43 kN. Rule: lowest trip > 2 × largest normal peak, and highest trip < 43 kN / 4 ≈ 10.8 kN. Sol calls the factors "preliminary judgement from memory".
3. **What holds the halves shut until then.** Latch dogs (hooks across the split). Fable: the first 5 mm of travel pulls them back. Sol: the shuttle's cams pull them back. Grok does not say.
4. **The first second.**
   - Closed on a centred 52 mm rope, each half is pulled onto the rope with about 1 030 N. The magnets push across the split with about 370 N. Net, about 550–700 N holds the halves together (correction; used by 3 of 3).
   - What happens as the gap grows has **not been calculated** (3 of 3).
   - The wedge or cam must break that grip, driven by the snag's push. Fable estimates 1–3 kN of axial force for this. Fable also says the rope supplies about 500 J where 30–100 J is needed.
   - Springs finish the travel. Fable: about 200–300 N per arm. Grok: sized above the measured force–gap curve. Sol: springs complete the travel; the mechanism must overcome about 1.03 kN per half and 370 N, plus friction and margin.
   - **Do the halves come off by themselves? No, 3 of 3.**
   - **Fable's warning (Fable only):** the head floats sideways. Pushing the halves apart from each other does not push both off the rope. One half can stay on (about 1 kN) while the carrier shifts and the other half travels double. No design reacts the opening against something fixed. Fable is sure of the mechanics, not of how often it would happen. Only Fable's concept drives the carrier onto its float stop, so the second half is stripped off against the structure.
   - Fable adds a one-way catch, so the head cannot close again if the snag lets go half-way.
5. **How they are held.** Each half stays on its arm (3 of 3) and goes into a park latch. Fable: a latch that needs a tool to undo (taken from Grok's step 1). Grok and Sol add a tether as backup.
6. **Debris.** None intended (3 of 3): pin ends stay in their pockets (Grok), breaking parts captive (Fable), nothing crushes or sheds (Sol). Nothing is meant to fall into the lower block (Grok).
7. **What can still go wrong (Grok).** The concept still does not guarantee "never stop the hoist". A seized shuttle, a spring that cannot climb the real curve, or a normal side load inside the pin band will stop the hoist or, worse, fail to release. Grok's kill test: if rope tension × angle inside the normal envelope reaches the 8 kN hold.
8. **Reporting.** "Closed" and "parked clear" per half (3 of 3). A wire loop through the pin goes open when it breaks (Grok, Fable). A cut cable reads as a fault (Grok, Fable). Sol: "clear" must be proven within a tested time limit. Fable: a load pin that records the trip force, on prototypes only.
9. **Putting it back.** Hoist stopped. Inspect and dress the rope, close with a screw tool, fit a new pin (3 of 3). Sol: proof-test both directions. Fable: once a year, pull the pin, stroke the head by lever and measure the opening force.

## 6. Centring and mounting

- **Grok:** one station of four polymer-tyred rollers on plain bushes, outside the sensor plane, in a free two-axis gimbal on links with sealed ball joints. The gimbal takes the angle. Roller stiffness must beat the rope's pull off centre on the 52 mm head, which is not known yet.
- **Fable:** two stations of four rollers at 90°, polymer tyres, plain bushes, non-magnetic axles, on sprung arms of about 0.2–0.3 kN/mm. Fable's estimate of the centring error is about 0.25 mm. Mounted on links with ball joints, free sideways and in tilt, with a follow force under about 100 N. Reason: only even, sprung contact stays centred as the rope thins 2–3 % (from memory, Fable step 1).
- **Sol:** two stations of four broad polymer-tyred rollers at 90°, a two-axis gimbal and a sealed sideways float. Reason: two stations set both the rope's position and its angle through the sensor plane.
- **Sliding liners only:** not discussed in step 2. In step 1, Grok rejected them (the rope walks to the bore wall) and Sol listed a full-length liner as an alternative.
- All three note that the rope's pull off centre is now about 0.12 kN/mm, a figure for the smaller head. The 52 mm value is missing.

## 7. What has been done before (all from memory, not checked)

- Portable testers are split heads, put round the rope for a survey and then taken off (Grok). They are hung on slings so they line up with the rope, and an operator stops the rope (Fable). They use wear shoes, bell mouths, rollers and a jointed suspension (Sol). From memory, not checked.
- No tester with an automatic snag release is known to Grok or Fable. Grok: "I do not remember a device" in this family that relies on its own weight to fall clear of a magnetised rope. From memory, not checked.
- Rope guides (fairleads) use a long flare or rollers. They guide but do not release (Grok, Sol). From memory, not checked.
- Rope lubricators: a clamshell collar on a moving rope, held only by straps (Fable). From memory, not checked.
- Detachable ropeway grips are forced open by a fixed rail or cam, not by the grip letting go (3 of 3). Opening is monitored, and a grip that stays shut is an emergency stop (Grok). From memory, not checked.
- Wireline tools have a weak point set between the normal pull and the cable's strength (3 of 3). Fable: a tension link, not a shear bolt. From memory, not checked.
- Shear pins on drives keep their broken pieces inside the coupling (Grok). Break-away guards use lanyards (Sol). From memory, not checked.
- Crane anti-two-block weight: a loose ring on the rope with a switch (Fable). From memory, not checked.

## 8. Errors the models found in each other

- **Fable step 1, "held closed only by their magnets and a light detent".** Flagged by 3 of 3. Grok and Sol: the magnets repel across the split, so they are not the clamp. **Corrected:** Fable's final uses latch dogs.
- **Grok step 1, a ramp of 2.6 kN that "cracks the magnetic grip", then light springs.** The three judge it differently:
  - Grok: 2.6 kN is fair arithmetic and beats the closed net, but light springs will not finish the stroke.
  - Fable: the mechanics are wrong ("a wedge gives F·tan or F/tan, less friction"), the ramp acts only while the pin deforms, then light springs stall against about 1 kN per half.
  - Sol: ideal geometry only; friction, pin deformation and loss of snag force are left out.
  - **Corrected:** Grok now uses a wedge plus springs sized to the curve.
- **Grok step 1, pins across the split.** An axial pull puts no shear on them, so they would never break (Fable). **Corrected in effect:** Grok's pin now holds the shuttle.
- **Withdrawn 0.4–0.8 kN/mm, used by:**
  - Grok: gravity at 1 mm offset, 1.6–3.2 kN at the wall, 1 kN roller preload.
  - Fable: V-shoe seating; "springs need kN-level preloads".
  - Sol: 0.2–0.4 kN at 0.5 mm.
  - **All corrected in step 2.** The gravity conclusion still holds through the 550–700 N net. Fable dropped the shoes because "the preload objection has gone".
- **Fable step 1, pull of 0.3 kN per half.** Too low (3 of 3; the corrected figure is about 1 030 N). Fable: low by a factor of 2–3 against 577 N (smaller head) and about 1 030 N. Its cross-check figure of 0.25 kN/mm should be 0.12 by its own numbers (Grok). Corrected.
- **Fable step 1, funnel angle "below the friction angle or a wire tip stubs".** Grok and Fable: the rule runs the wrong way round (below the friction angle is the self-locking condition). Sol: a wire is a bending cantilever, and no single friction-angle rule decides it. The angle may still be right (Grok, Fable).
- **Grok step 1, rope breaking force as the yardstick.** Grok called a wire bunch "still small against a 2 400 kN rope". Sol: wrong criterion; local wire or strand damage and the allowable hoist overload can be far below rope break.
- **Fable step 1, 80 mm opening against ±70 mm slide plus birdcage radius.** It fails Fable's own rule (Grok, Fable).
- **Grok step 1, "four rollers set two axes in two planes".** Four rollers in one plane do not set the rope's angle (Grok, Sol). Fable reads the layout differently: one axis per plane, so the rope is free in the other axis at each end. Grok still keeps one station.
- **Sol step 1, spring catch at 5.0 ± 0.5 kN.** It will not hold its band for years in salt and ice, and it sits inside the ice loads (Grok, Fable). **Corrected:** Sol now uses a pin.
- **Sol step 1, backup links.** If the shuttle seizes, breaking the links opens nothing (Fable).
- **Sol step 1, 10 mm separation in 50 ms.** At 3 m/min the rope moves only 2.5 mm in 50 ms (Grok, Fable).
- **Sol step 1, alarm with no "parked clear" proof.** A half left on the rope gets dragged into the sheave (Grok, Sol). Corrected.
- **Sol step 1, relief slots in the funnel.** They give a wire tip an edge to catch on (Fable, Sol).

## 9. To calculate or measure before anything is drawn

Each test is listed with the result that would kill the concept. Sol's warning for all of them: the rope's breaking force (2 400 kN) is the wrong yardstick. Local wire or strand damage and the allowable hoist overload can be far below it.

- **Grok:**
  1. Force against opening gap, 52 mm rope, closed to 80 mm. Kill if a wedge plus springs that fit the head cannot meet the peak force or the work in time (before the rope moves one funnel length at 0.25 m/s).
  2. Force against offset on the 52 mm head, to at least 2 mm. Kill if holding 0.5 mm needs a roller load that cuts wire crowns in the wear trial.
  3. Tension, wander, angle, nearest sheave. Kill if tension × angle inside the normal envelope reaches the 8 kN hold, or no position keeps the float off its stops.
  4. Pin break load at −40 °C and +50 °C after salt, with overshoot and fatigue. Kill if the band cannot stay above normal load and at or under about 11 kN.
  5. Funnel tests with damaged rope, both directions, 3 and 15 m/min. Kill if a single wire trips the pin, or the shoe cuts the rope before the head is clear.
  6. Opening time on a real magnetised head. Kill if a half is still within a rope diameter after the rope has moved one funnel length, or one half parks and the other stalls.
  7. Iced and salted release at −40 °C. Kill if the trip can freeze solid above the pin load.
  8. Roller wear. Kill if centring leaves the 0.5 mm band inside a maintenance interval the site will keep.
- **Fable:**
  1. 3D model for the 35, 52 and 57 mm heads (force against gap, one half alone, empty head, offset, pull when parked). Kill if stripping a half needs more than about 3 kN, or the pull is still above about 300 N at a 30 mm gap.
  2. Site tension, movement and vibration. Kill if no position is within about ±100 mm and ±5°, or strong excitation sits near the head-on-rope resonance (roughly 30 Hz, Fable's estimate).
  3. Centring budget. Kill if more than about 0.5 kN per roller is needed, or the error exceeds 0.5 mm.
  4. Cold chamber at −40 °C. Kill if normal peaks exceed about 3 kN.
  5. Damaged samples both directions; lifted strand pulled to 15 kN. Kill if the strand is damaged further below 15 kN, or wires stub repeatedly.
  6. Pin scatter, salted, −40 and +50 °C. Kill if the band is wider than 6–15 kN.
  7. Full release rig, including one half sticking. Kill if any run leaves a half inside the rope envelope.
  8. Wear and sensor response. Kill if wire crowns are marked, or tyre life is shorter than the service interval.
- **Sol:**
  1. Site survey (tension, drag, movement, park space). Kill if no clear park space exists, or normal peaks leave no release window below damaging load.
  2. 3D magnetic model plus force rig, including no-rope cases. Kill if practical cams and springs cannot overcome the pull.
  3. Release window with the springiness of the rope and bracket. Kill if lowest trip > 2 × largest normal peak and highest trip < 43 kN / 4 cannot both be met (factors from memory).
  4. Snag tests, both directions and speeds. Kill if entry worsens defects, peak load exceeds limits, or either half fails to clear.
  5. Environment (grease, salt, ice, −40…+50 °C). Kill if any credible single fault prevents forced opening or clear-state reporting.
  6. Endurance. Kill if ±0.5 mm cannot be held at an acceptable maintenance interval.
  7. Full-hoist trial. Kill if release overloads the rope or structure, or cannot reliably let a lift finish after confirmed clearance.

## 10. Questions to us

These are the step 1 questions, merged; step 2 asked none.

1. What is the working rope tension, and what extra force trips the drive or may the head put on rope and structure? (all)
2. At the head's position: sideways travel, angle and vibration, empty and full? How close is the nearest sheave? May we put the head near the upper block? (all)
3. After a clear release, may the lift finish on an alarm? May the next lift run unmonitored? (all)
4. When will 3D force results (against opening gap and against offset) exist for the 52 mm head? (Grok, Sol)
5. Is 0.5 mm an absolute centre or a pass-to-pass repeat? Are the sensor channels summed? (Fable, Grok)
6. Must any splice, clip or other enlargement pass the head? What is the largest? (Grok)
7. Wire, strand and real rope diameters, and their tolerance? (Grok, Fable)
8. Is there room for the funnels, about 75 mm travel per half (Sol's step 1 figure, dropped in step 2) and the arms? Can we run destructive snag tests? (Sol)

## 11. What the round cannot settle

- How real broken wires behave at the funnel after the rope reverses. This also decides the funnel angle and material.
- The force curve as the halves open, the 52 mm off-centre stiffness, and the pull on a parked half.
- Ice on this rope, and whether pins, shafts and latches still move after years of salt.
- Long-term roller and rope wear.
- How often a release happens, and whether operations accept finishing a lift on an alarm.

## Summary writer's doubts

- Grok accepts pins up to 12 kN, but its own kill test asks for "at or under about 11 kN".
- Sol rejects Fable's geometric mean as meaningless. Sol's own "equal factors between 1.5 and 43 kN" gives the same kind of middle (√(1.5 × 43) ≈ 8 kN).
- Grok keeps "at least 80 mm clear" after saying 80 mm fails against the float plus the birdcage radius.
- Grok keeps one roller station after agreeing that four rollers in one plane do not set the angle; it relies on the gimbal.
