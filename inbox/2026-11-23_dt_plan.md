# Plan after the restart: the digital twin (DT) of the SWS

Kai, Mon 23 Nov 2026. Built on a read-only pass through everything written on the twin. Nothing here is
started. Each step needs your go.

## Where we stand, in short

- **What exists:** a simulator of the whole data line on one PC (rope and head simulated; half, root box,
  transport and logger are real logic), the half in C, and a page that replays 12 recorded runs.
- **What it is not yet:** it is one head. Every head gets the same rope and the same fault list. So "pick
  rope 17 and put a fault on it" is not possible today.
- **The signal is a formula, not FEM.** Pulse shapes, ripple shape and noise are assumptions with sizes taken
  from the FEM notes. No measured recording has ever been played through it.
- **The MAx zoom view runs on demo data only** (every value is 100). There is no twin code behind it.
- **The view with too many winches is found:** the zoom chart's scale card assumes about 250 SWS, which gives
  "~125 winches" and about 42 base stations. It also draws 2 base stations x 3 winches. The right figures are
  3 stations x 17 winches = 51 winches, 102 SWS, 204 halves.

## The steps, in order

### 1. Look at the data (no fitting yet)

- You give me the lab recording from the prototype and the data sheets of the ADC. The data sheets are read,
  never copied into the project repository.
- A builder reads the format and reports: channels, rate, length, units, what was on the rope.
- Plots by the plot standard: raw traces, spectrum, noise floor, strand ripple (size and pitch), mains hum,
  drift, the standing levels, and any fault passes.
- One table: **what the twin assumes / what was measured / the ratio**, for every setting below.

| Setting in the twin today | Value |
|---|---|
| Noise per sensing element | 1.5 µT rms, white only |
| Strand ripple, radial / axial | 265 / 105 µT, pitch 40 mm |
| Broken outer wire, radial / axial | 1.35 / 0.156 mT per wire |
| Loss of metal, axial | -0.675 mT per % |
| Standing field, radial / axial | 50 mT (assumed) / 5 mT |
| Drift | 20 µT per hour |
| Mains hum | 30 µT at 49.9 Hz |

### 2. Fit the twin to the data

- Replace the assumed values with measured ones. Add what is missing: low-frequency noise (the twin has white
  noise only), measured pulse and ripple shapes.
- Compare the FEM model of the prototype head (already made from the drawing) with the measurement: standing
  field and signal sizes. The correction found there is what we carry to the 52 mm head.
- Play the real recording through the real line code (half, root box, logger). This has never been done.
- Re-run the one proof that fails today: position from ripple, 27.3 mm against a 10 mm target, now with real
  ripple.
- Once the reference-instrument measurement of the same rope is in, it gives the second dimension.

### 3. One page of fixed facts for the DT, and the contradictions cleared

The older twin documents disagree with what you have ruled. Before a full-scale twin is built, one page states
the facts, and the old sources are corrected or marked as superseded. The ones that need your ruling:

- **Where is the processing: in the root box (specification) or at the logger (the newer notes and the
  simulator)?**
- The specification still says the root box adds position from an encoder. You have ruled: no encoder.
- The specification says 220 m of rope and 3 / 15 m/min. The simulator uses 250 m and 3.2 / 5 m/min.
- Who stamps the time: the half, or the root box.
- Old figures to remove: 24 channels at 100 per second, three converters in the zoom chart, 0.5 °C against
  0.01 °C temperature resolution, "base station" against "root box".

### 4. The full-scale DT for the 52 mm installation

- Every rope gets its own identity: 51 ropes, each with its own fault list and history; 102 SWS with their own
  noise; 204 halves.
- The structure is the MAx zoom: shiplift -> 3 main stations -> 17 winches each -> 2 SWS -> 2 halves. The
  chart's counts are corrected at the same time.
- The twin writes to the same server path as the real system will (the root boxes publish, the logger takes
  it), so the HMI cannot tell twin from real.
- Bending cycles come in as a second source, as from the control system, to the same server.

### 5. Three screens, kept apart

- **Our HMI (main):** the design from the Opus version, with your rulings: Concern in orange, findings latch,
  the worst value drops when a finding is dismissed, no "6 d" in the header. Fed live from the twin. It does
  not change when the trainer is used.
- **Trainer HMI:** pick a rope, a position and a fault type, and create it in the twin. Also line faults
  (lost frames, network stop, a half restarting). Separate page, separate address.
- **Customer HMI:** later.

### 6. The platform steel, 4 m down to 40 cm

- On record already: steel within about half a metre shifts the reading by 1 to 8 % of metal. Nothing on
  record about a structure that moves towards the head. The twin has no term for surroundings at all.
- A 3D run: the standing field at the sensing elements against the distance to the structure, from 4 m to
  0.4 m, and the same for the drum with one layer of rope.
- If it shows: a surroundings term in the twin, driven by platform position, and a note on what it means for
  the zero.
- **I need from you:** rough size and shape of the structure, which side of the SWS it passes, and how the
  SWS sits relative to the drum.

### 7. Firmware update and the connection to each root box

What the record says today:
- The specification requires a bootloader with remote firmware update through the root box, and lists
  "firmware distribution to the halves" as a root box task, with a test for it.
- A proposal exists for the update message: the image carries its own check sum, checked before the switch.
- Each root box has one network connection (one switch port per root box); the root box is the only node on
  the network; each half has its own line to it.
- **Nothing is written** on updating the root box's own software, on version numbering, or on what happens
  when an update fails halfway. Nothing of it is built in the simulator.

So this is a design job: a short design note, then update scenarios in the twin (good update, broken image,
power lost mid-update, one half on an old version).
