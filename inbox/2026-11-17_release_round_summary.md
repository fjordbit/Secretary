# Release round 17 Nov: summary

Three models (Grok 4.7, Fable 5.2, Sol = GPT 5.6) each designed the head's entry, release and centring (step 1). Then each cross-checked all three answers without names (step 2). Step 2 corrected the task: across the split the magnets **push the halves apart**, and each half is pulled onto the rope. The old 0.4–0.8 kN per mm side force is withdrawn. This is the models' opinion; the mechanical designer decides.

Words: kN = kilonewton, about 100 kg of force. Shuttle = a short slide that lets the head move a little along the rope. Wedge = a slope that turns that movement into a sideways push. Gimbal = two pivots so the head can tilt.

## 1. In five lines

1. The models think the halves will not come off the rope by themselves. They propose a mechanism that pushes them off and latches them clear. (3 of 3)
2. They end on a captive breaking pin, not a spring catch. Grok and Fable propose 10 kN, Sol 8 kN.
3. They propose that the snag's drag moves the head on its shuttle, and a wedge turns this into a push that opens the halves. Springs finish, the halves latch and stay tied. (3 of 3) **But Fable warns: pushing the halves apart does not push both off the rope. One half can stay on. Only Fable's concept pushes the opening against something fixed.**
4. They propose a funnel at each end and no crush funnel, a head that floats and tilts with the rope, and rollers to centre it. One roller station or two is disputed.
5. First before any drawing: the force against opening gap on the 52 mm rope (Grok), the 3D magnetic model for three head sizes (Fable), a site survey of tension and rope movement (Sol).

## 2. Agreed by 3 of 3

- Split funnel at each end, no step into the bore. A standing wire is laid down, not caught.
- No crush funnel. It leaves debris and leaves the head on the rope.
- Gravity cannot clear a half: about 70 N weight against a 550–700 N net hold (correction).
- Captive breaking pin. Its load is set by its size, not by friction.
- Release load between 1.5 kN (one wire) and 43 kN (smallest strand catch), both directions.
- The head follows the rope. A stiff bracket fights tension × angle and trips by mistake.
- Rollers and funnels leave with the halves. Nothing stays around the rope.
- Each half reports "closed" and "parked clear". "Not closed" does not prove clear.
- Both clear: latched alarm, the lift may finish, the next start is blocked.
- Reset with the hoist stopped: inspect the rope, close with a screw tool, new pin.
- V-shoes rejected, also by Fable, who proposed them. They use up the 0.5 mm budget.
- An empty head (no rope) is not calculated and may push itself open.

## 3. Agreed by 2 of 3

- **10 kN pin** (Grok 8–12 kN, Fable hold ≥6, break ≤15 kN). **Sol: 8 kN, 7–9 kN**, about equal margins to 1.5 and 43 kN; valid only if normal peaks stay below 3.5 kN.
- **Two roller stations, four rollers each** (Fable, Sol). **Grok: one station**; the gimbal sets the angle, and two stations in a tilting head fight each other.
- **Plastic funnel face on a non-magnetic backing, about 100 mm mouth, 100 mm long** (Grok, Sol; Grok: UHMWPE on stainless, about 11°). **Fable: aluminium bronze, 12°**; after reversal a wire tip digs into plastic.
- **Plain bushes, no ball bearings** (Grok, Fable). **Sol** objects: the claim ignores 15 m/min and years of salt and ice.
- **Float on sealed ball-joint links, no slides** (Grok, Fable): slides ice up. **Sol**: "sealed lateral float", type not stated.
- **Latch dogs (hooks across the split) hold the halves shut** (Fable, Sol). **Grok** does not say what holds them.
- **A half that does not report clear: stop** (Grok, Sol). **Fable**: stop only if drag continues; the owner decides.

## 4. Disputed and left open

- Opening speed: nearly static (Fable); a fast jolt when the pin breaks is possible (Sol); about 0.3 s at 15 m/min (Grok).
- Park clearance: at least 80 mm (Grok); more than 80 mm (Fable); from measured movement (Sol).
- Closure strength. Fable rates it "30 kN or more" (bracket too, step 1). Sol: below the 43 kN strand catch. Fable's reason for a strong closure: a swell wedged in the funnel pushes outward about 2.5 × the axial force (Fable's estimate), so the split carries the 370 N push plus this burst.
- Head position near the upper block (Fable); open (Grok, Sol).

## 5. The release, step by step

1. **First to go:** a captive pin holding the shuttle (3 of 3); in double shear (Grok, Sol). Fable adds a "dragging" switch at 3 mm of travel.
2. **Load, the models' estimates:**
   - Grok: 10 kN, 8–12 kN. Hold one wire and a small bunch; gone before 43 kN; 5 kN is too close to one wire plus ice. (Step 1: overshoot to about 15 kN still under 43 kN.)
   - Fable: about 10 kN, hold ≥6, break ≤15 kN. Its step 1 ice estimates (2–3 kN scraping, up to 17 kN frozen on) crowd 5 kN.
   - Sol: 8 kN, 7–9 kN. Lowest trip > 2 × largest normal peak; highest trip < 43 kN / 4 ≈ 10.8 kN ("preliminary judgement from memory").
3. **Halves unlocked:** the latch dogs are pulled back by the first 5 mm of travel (Fable) or by the cams (Sol).
4. **First second:**
   - Closed on a centred 52 mm rope, each half is pulled onto the rope with about 1 030 N. The magnets push across the split with about 370 N. Net about 550–700 N holds the halves together (correction, 3 of 3).
   - The force as the gap grows is **not calculated** (3 of 3).
   - The wedge, driven by the snag, breaks the grip. Fable: 1–3 kN axial needed; the rope gives about 500 J where 30–100 J is needed.
   - Springs finish: about 200–300 N per arm (Fable); sized above the measured curve (Grok); must overcome about 1.03 kN and 370 N plus margin (Sol).
   - **Do the halves come off by themselves? No, 3 of 3.**
   - **Fable's warning (Fable only):** the head floats, so pushing the halves apart does not push both off the rope. One half can stay on (about 1 kN) while the other travels double. No design reacts the opening against something fixed. Only Fable's concept drives the carrier onto its float stop to strip the second half. Fable also adds a one-way catch against re-closing.
5. **Held:** each half on its arm, into a park latch (3 of 3). Latch needs a tool (Fable, from Grok's step 1). Backup tether (Grok, Sol).
6. **Debris:** none intended; pin parts captive (3 of 3). Nothing to fall into the lower block (Grok).
7. **What can still go wrong (Grok):** "never stop the hoist" is still not guaranteed. A seized shuttle, a spring that cannot climb the real curve, or a normal side load inside the pin band will stop the hoist or, worse, fail to release. Kill if tension × angle inside the normal envelope reaches the 8 kN hold.
8. **Reported:** "closed" and "parked clear" per half (3 of 3). Wire loop through the pin; cut cable = fault (Grok, Fable). Clear proven within a tested time (Sol).
9. **Put back:** hoist stopped, rope inspected, screw tool, new pin (3 of 3). Proof-test both directions (Sol). Yearly opening-force test (Fable).

## 6. Centring and mounting

- **Grok:** one station, four plastic-tyred rollers on plain bushes, in a free gimbal on ball-joint links. The gimbal takes the angle.
- **Fable:** two stations of four rollers at 90°, sprung arms about 0.2–0.3 kN/mm, estimated error about 0.25 mm. Only even sprung contact stays centred as the rope thins 2–3 % (from memory).
- **Sol:** two stations of four rollers at 90°, gimbal and sideways float. Two stations set both position and angle.
- Sliding liners: not discussed in step 2. Off-centre pull is now about 0.12 kN/mm (smaller head); the 52 mm value is missing.

## 7. Done before (all from memory, not checked)

- Portable testers are put on for a survey and taken off; none with an automatic snag release is known (Grok, Fable).
- Rope guides guide but do not release (Grok, Sol).
- Ropeway grips are forced open by a rail or cam (3 of 3).
- Wireline tools have a weak point between normal pull and cable strength (3 of 3).

## 8. Errors found in each other

- Fable's halves "held closed only by their magnets" (3 of 3; Grok, Sol: the magnets repel). Corrected: latch dogs.
- Grok's 2.6 kN ramp plus light springs. Grok: springs will not finish. Fable: wrong mechanics, springs stall. Sol: friction left out. Corrected: wedge plus springs.
- The withdrawn 0.4–0.8 kN/mm was used by all three. Corrected in step 2; the gravity conclusion still holds.
- Fable's 0.3 kN pull per half was too low (3 of 3); Fable: 2–3× against 577 N and about 1 030 N. Corrected.
- Sol's 5.0 ± 0.5 kN spring catch will not hold its band (Grok, Fable). Corrected: pin.

## 9. To calculate or measure first, with what kills the concept

Sol's warning: the rope's 2 400 kN breaking force is the wrong yardstick. Wire or strand damage and the allowable hoist overload can be far below it.

- **Grok:**
  1. Force against opening gap, 52 mm rope, to 80 mm. Kill if a wedge plus springs that fit cannot meet it in time.
  2. Force against offset, 52 mm head, to at least 2 mm. Kill if holding 0.5 mm needs a roller load that cuts wire crowns.
  3. Tension, wander, angle, nearest sheave. Kill if tension × angle reaches the 8 kN hold.
  4. Pin at −40 °C and +50 °C after salt. Kill if the band cannot stay above normal load and at or under about 11 kN.
  5. Damaged-rope funnel tests, both directions, 3 and 15 m/min. Kill if one wire trips the pin, or the shoe cuts the rope before the head is clear.
  6. Opening time on a real head. Kill if one half parks and the other stalls.
  7. Iced release at −40 °C. Kill if the trip can freeze above the pin load.
  8. Roller wear. Kill if centring leaves 0.5 mm inside the maintenance interval.
- **Fable:**
  1. 3D model for 35, 52 and 57 mm heads. Kill if stripping a half needs more than about 3 kN, or the pull is above about 300 N at 30 mm gap.
  2. Site movement and vibration. Kill if no position is within about ±100 mm and ±5°, or excitation sits near roughly 30 Hz (Fable's estimate).
  3. Centring budget. Kill if more than about 0.5 kN per roller is needed.
  4. Cold chamber at −40 °C. Kill if normal peaks exceed about 3 kN.
  5. Damaged samples; strand pulled to 15 kN. Kill if the strand is damaged further below 15 kN, or wires stub repeatedly.
  6. Pin scatter. Kill if the band is wider than 6–15 kN.
  7. Full release rig. Kill if any run leaves a half inside the rope envelope.
  8. Wear and sensor response. Kill if wire crowns are marked.
- **Sol:**
  1. Site survey. Kill if no park space, or no release window below damaging load.
  2. 3D magnetic model and force rig, with no-rope cases. Kill if cams and springs cannot overcome the pull.
  3. Release window with rope and bracket springiness. Kill if the 2× and ÷4 limits cannot both be met.
  4. Snag tests. Kill if entry worsens defects, peak load exceeds limits, or a half fails to clear.
  5. Environment. Kill if one credible fault prevents opening or clear reporting.
  6. Endurance. Kill if ±0.5 mm cannot be held.
  7. Full-hoist trial. Kill if release overloads the rope or structure.

## 11. What the round cannot settle

- Broken wires at the funnel after reversal, and so funnel angle and material.
- The opening force curve and the 52 mm off-centre stiffness.
- Ice, salt ageing and long-term wear.
- Whether operations accept finishing a lift on an alarm.

## Summary writer's doubts

- Grok accepts pins up to 12 kN, but its own kill test asks for "at or under about 11 kN".
- Sol calls Fable's geometric mean meaningless, yet its own 8 kN is the geometric mean of 1.5 and 43 kN.
- Grok keeps 80 mm clearance and one roller station after criticising both.
