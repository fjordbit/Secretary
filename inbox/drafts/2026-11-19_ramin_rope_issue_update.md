**Status, and what we still need.**

We have not stopped for the missing rope data. Where the data is missing, the model runs a low, a middle and a high case inside what is physically possible, and each is marked as assumed. The note is here:
https://miniature-bassoon-zqw7owk.pages.github.io/sws/sensor.html#s-ldlma

What we hold today comes from the rope maker's catalogue: 18 compacted outer strands, a plastic-covered steel inner rope, metallic area factor 0.571. The steel's magnetic curve is assumed.

**What the new results show (calculated, not measured):**

- The principle for the 52 mm head now holds together: a groove round the yoke at the sensors gives a loss-of-metal reading about 4 times stronger than without it.
- The best field in the rope for that reading lies between 1.90 and 2.03 T. Where in that span depends on the rope steel, which we do not know.
- A rope 1 % thicker than nominal reads as about 2 % more metal. So the real diameter and metallic area matter directly.
- One broken wire in the inner rope gives a signal close to the noise limit. Its depth under the plastic and its diameter decide whether we see it.
- Steel within about half a metre of the head, and a neighbouring rope, change the reading.

So we can move forward with the three cases, but we want the real values before the design is finalised.

**What we need, in order of weight:**

1. **The certificate for the delivered 52 mm rope** (and for the 40 mm rope on the prototype rig): measured diameter, metallic area, wire tensile grade.
2. **Wire diameters** of the outer strands and of the inner rope, and the **thickness of the plastic** between them.
3. **Diameter tolerance** as delivered and under load.
4. **Magnetic data for the wire steel**, if the rope maker has any. If not: a short length of the rope or of its wires, so it can be measured.
5. **The surroundings at the lift:** the distance between neighbouring ropes where a head would sit, and what steel (sheaves, beams) is within half a metre of that place.

Points 1–3 replace assumptions already in the model. Points 4 and 5 are new, from this week's results.
